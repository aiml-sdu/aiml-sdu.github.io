Research

Interpretability and AI safety

We study how trained machine learning models work internally, mostly language models. We look for the parts of a model that produce a given behaviour, and at how those parts change when the model is fine-tuned. We also study the methods used for this kind of analysis and when their results can be trusted, drawing on philosophy of science and on neuroscience, which has long faced similar questions about the brain.

Our work on AI safety asks how much safety evaluations tell us. A fine-tuned model can behave well on test prompts and differently in ordinary use, and a defence against harmful fine-tuning can look effective for reasons unrelated to safety. We locate such gaps inside models and propose standards for when a safety claim counts as shown. We also ask what should be required before a model is deployed in areas such as health care.

Themes
  • Mechanistic interpretabilityWhere inside a model a behaviour comes from, and what training changes.
  • Philosophy of machine learningWhen an explanation, a measurement or a model’s account of itself can be believed.
  • AI safetyWhat safety tests, audits and defences against harmful fine-tuning measure, and what should be required before a model is deployed.
